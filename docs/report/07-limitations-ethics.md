# 7 · Limitations, safety and ethics

> **Draft 1, 2026-10-14.** `docs/04` §7 requires every report, presentation and demo to state six
> limitations: synthetic data with unknown performance on real patients; chest pain only; aortic
> dissection handled by rule, not learned; a closed world of 14 conditions; no clinical validation;
> and confidence values that are research estimates, not clinical probabilities. This chapter states
> them, the risks behind them, the safety design, and every disclosure the protocol requires.

## 7.1 Synthetic data, and what it cannot tell us

Every patient the system was trained and evaluated on is synthetic, generated by DDXPlus from a
rule-based simulator (§3.2). **Its performance on real patients is unknown**, and no result in this
report is a claim of clinical accuracy or efficacy (`docs/04` §1, F-6). The generator's structure
makes the data easier than real histories in specific ways:

- each patient's evidence is drawn only from their own condition's list, so on full histories the
  classifiers are near-perfect (R-16, §7.5);
- findings are drawn independently, so a patient can lack the defining finding of their own
  condition (20% of Boerhaave patients never report vomiting, EXP-008);
- the vocabulary follows DDXPlus's conventions, not always clinical ones: "tearing" pain belongs to
  pneumothorax and Boerhaave, and radiation to the jaw to pulmonary edema (§3.2.4);
- the English is machine-translated from French.

The hand-written golden cases are the only inputs not drawn from the generator. There are four of
them, so they show whether the system behaves as a clinician expects on a classic presentation,
not how often it is right.

## 7.2 Circularity (R-12)

The feasibility spike found BODHI-S too broad to be the graph's backbone (31% of conditions matched
exactly, against a 60% gate; EXP-001), so the knowledge graph is built from DDXPlus's own condition
knowledge base, the same source the ML ranker learns from and the source the patients were generated
from. **The graph is therefore not an independent source of knowledge on DDXPlus data**, and the
question "does the graph add value over ML alone?" is weakened (`docs/10`, R-12).

The size of the effect is visible: the graph alone, with no training, ranks the true condition first
for 0.923 of validation patients and reaches Precision@3 0.756, close to B1-XGB's 0.764 (EXP-005).
None of that is skill. Following `docs/05` §8 rule 7, **every graph figure on DDXPlus data in this
report carries the caveat**, and the graph's independent contributions are reported apart from its
shared-source part:

- **aortic dissection**, in neither DDXPlus nor the ML ranker, hand-authored from the ADD-RS and the
  IRAD registry (20 edges);
- **the red-flag rules**, written from published clinical patterns, not derived from DDXPlus;
- **BODHI-S's enrichment** of four conditions (56 edges).

Every edge records its source, so this separation is mechanical (`docs/02` §5.1). Even the
independent parts are judged partly on DDXPlus: the red-flag rates are upper bounds on what the
rules do with real histories (EXP-008). The predicted honest finding, written before any result, is
that the graph earns its place on safety and explainability rather than raw accuracy (`docs/10`);
the ablations will test it [pending: EXP-012, test split].

## 7.3 Bias and synthetic demographics

DDXPlus's demographics are a simulator's (EXP-002). Every one of the 13 conditions is about 50%
female, the median age of its MI patients is 45, and its spontaneous pneumothorax patients are 52%
female, none of which reflects real epidemiology [citation needed: real-world sex and age
distributions of MI and pneumothorax]. The model therefore learns no real sex or age priors. Two
consequences:

- **a near-equal result by sex is an artefact of generation, not evidence of fairness**, and will be
  reported as such (`docs/04` §6);
- sex differences in how acute coronary syndrome presents are clinically real (women more often
  present atypically) [citation needed: sex differences in ACS presentation], and a model trained on
  synthetic data may under-represent them, a safety concern we state rather than claim to have
  solved.

BODHI-S is India- and SNOMED-tagged, and its vocabulary may carry regional framing (`docs/04` §6).
Per-condition and by-sex breakdowns will be reported where the sample allows, and fairness will not
be claimed where it has not been measured [pending: EXP-012, test split].

## 7.4 The case-to-token inversion has no ground truth (R-17), and A-10

A hand-written case reaches the model through a hand-curated choice of which DDXPlus answer each
concept stands for (§4.8). No dataset of (hand-written case, DDXPlus tokens) pairs exists, so these
choices **cannot be validated against anything**, and a wrong one is silent: downstream it looks
exactly like a model error (R-17).

**A-10 is that risk realised.** The 17 representative answers were reviewed and approved as they
were on 2026-10-12 (who reviewed them is not recorded). On 2026-10-13 the model's reasons showed the
transformer, on GC-001 alone, answering acute pulmonary edema at 0.9997, with about +15 log-odds on
one finding, radiation to the jaw or arm. The concept had been translated into `E_57_@_V_121`
(*mâchoire*, the jaw), which in DDXPlus **only acute pulmonary edema patients give**; DDXPlus's MI and
angina patients radiate *under* the jaw (`V_163`). The answer was clinically sensible and wrong for
the data. The owner chose `V_163` (decision A-10); afterwards GC-001 alone ranks unstable angina
first under both models, and every golden case passes with every model, as it did before (EXP-021).
Three points must be stated with it:

- it was **found by looking at the transformer on a golden case**, and the record says so; the
  golden expectations were not changed;
- **the other 16 answers have not been checked** against which conditions give them in DDXPlus, so
  R-17 stays open (`docs/07`);
- the safeguards that remain are the ones that found it: every token records the concept it came
  from, every dropped finding keeps its reason, the tests round-trip every concept through the
  crosswalk's forward direction, and the model's reasons name the finding a ranking rests on.

The same class of judgement underlies decision A-8 (narrower matches admitted, so sudden onset and
exertional pain reach the model) and decision A-5 (sudden onset means an onset speed of at least 8
of 10).

## 7.5 Saturation (R-16), and what the hypotheses can show

On full DDXPlus histories B1 ranks the true condition in the top 3 for every validation patient and
keeps every must-not-miss condition there (EXP-004). **H1, H2 and the first-written Target therefore
cannot be supported at 100%**: the test cannot tell whether fusion helps. The protocol's response,
amendment 3, added the 50% and 25% levels. But the models trained with masked copies saturate those
too: at 25% they reach top-3 0.997–0.998 and must-not-miss recall@3 0.996–0.998, and at 50% they are
perfect or within four misses (EXP-019, §5.9). As approved, the reduced-evidence claims cannot be met
on these validation figures, and the team has been asked again (E-2).

We report this as a limitation of the benchmark, not a result about the architecture. What still
separates systems is top-1 and MRR at 25%, agreement with the differential (Precision@3, Recall@5,
with the R-12 caveat), aortic dissection, the golden cases and the explanations. The deep ranker's
gain is small for the same reason (R-19): at 25% its MRR is 0.0018 above logistic regression's on
validation (EXP-021).

## 7.6 Random masks are not history-taking

The reduced-evidence levels remove a patient's answers **at random**, by question. A clinician does
not ask at random: they ask the questions that discriminate. These results therefore measure
**robustness to missing answers**, and must not be presented as a simulation of a consultation
(`docs/05` §8 rule 8). The mask also cannot move a patient's kept answers outside their own
condition's DDXPlus list, so the cause of R-16 is weakened at reduced evidence, not removed. A
history taken to answer the most informative questions first would likely look different from both
the full record and a random subset ["what to ask next", Phase 3d, pending].

## 7.7 The closed world (R-13), and what a probability means

The system knows 14 conditions and has no notion of "none of the above". A chest-pain presentation
caused by anything else, pneumonia for example, is still ranked as one of the 14, possibly with
confidence (R-13). Only 25.6% of DDXPlus's patients fall inside the 13 trainable conditions (EXP-002),
and the ground-truth differentials put a third of their probability mass on conditions the system
cannot output (EXP-013, decision D-8). Three things follow:

- **every probability is conditional on the patient having one of the 14 conditions**; DDXPlus's
  patients always do, and real patients may not (EXP-007, `src/fusion/calibration.py`);
- the API lists the 14 conditions, which are all it can rank (`GET /conditions`), and R-13's
  mitigation requires the UI to say so too [to verify in the dashboard before submission];
- the red-flag rules fire independently of the ranking, so the safety layer is not closed-world in
  the same way.

**What a displayed number means.** The models are calibrated against DDXPlus (raw ECE ≤ 0.011 at
every level, EXP-007), but near-certain on hand-written cases where a history cannot decide: on
GC-001 they give unstable angina 0.98–1.00 and MI almost 0, though only a troponin can separate them
(EXP-018, EXP-019). No calibration method fitted on DDXPlus fixes that (EXP-007). So **no raw score is
shown as a probability**: the dashboard shows ranks, the model's reasons are named without numbers,
and whether the fused probability is shown, as a number or as a band with a floor ("< 1%" rather than
"0%"), is decided with EXP-007's stage 2 [pending: EXP-007 stage 2]. Isotonic regression, one of the
planned methods, gave the true condition under 0.001 for up to 8.7% of patients at 25%, which is why
a displayed "0%" beside a must-not-miss condition is treated as a failure (EXP-007, a post hoc count).

## 7.8 Limits of the red flags and the safety layer

- **Sensitivity is below target**: 0.823 at 100% against 0.95, capped by DDXPlus omitting defining
  findings, and the rules were deliberately not loosened to chase it (EXP-008).
- **The rules collapse at reduced evidence** (0.327 at 50%, 0.135 at 25%), because most need two or
  three findings together; on partial histories safety rests on the ranker (EXP-019).
- **A tearing-pain flag fires on pneumothorax and Boerhaave in DDXPlus**, and the dissection flag is
  appropriate for dissection only (A-7), so those flags count against its precision (`docs/04` §3).
- **The fusion can rank dissection 2nd or 3rd** for presentations that score 0 on the ADD-RS but
  involve back pain or hypertension; whether that is clinically wanted is an open question for a
  clinician (A-2, `docs/02` §6).
- **The panel that decided A-7 was not fully blind** to the rules' firing rates, which the project
  notes state (`docs/04` §3).
- **The safety check is lexical.** It catches the treatment phrasing a template or a language model
  would use, not every paraphrase; checking claims against retrieved evidence is Phase 3c's
  [pending: 3c guardrails].
- **The reasons judge one finding at a time**, so two findings that matter only together, as most
  red-flag patterns do, can each look weak (`src/ml/reasons.py`).

## 7.9 The safety design

**Intended use** (`docs/04` §1). A research prototype providing decision support to qualified medical
staff evaluating an acute chest-pain presentation. It is **not** intended for autonomous diagnosis,
for patients or the public, for treatment, drug or dosing recommendations, for triage without a
clinician's review, or for real clinical care. It is not a medical device: it has had no clinical
validation, no regulatory review and no approval by any authority (CDSCO, FDA, EU MDR or other), and
no claim of clinical efficacy may appear in the report, the UI or any presentation.

**The disclaimer** is attached to every result, word for word, and may not be collapsible,
dismissible or less prominent than the ranking (`docs/04` §2). The safety layer restores it if a
result lacks it (§4.11).

**The must-not-miss policy** (`docs/04` §3): *"The cost of ranking a fatal condition too low is not
symmetric with the cost of ranking a benign condition too high."* So must-not-miss recall is the
headline metric; red-flag rules run independently of the ranker; the system is deliberately biased
toward flagging danger, accepting lower red-flag precision; and **absent is not unknown**: a denied
finding lowers a condition, and a finding not asked about must not.

**Table 7.1 — Failure modes and their mitigations** (`docs/04` §4), with their status on 2026-10-14.

| # | Failure mode | Mitigation | Status |

**Data ethics and privacy** (`docs/04` §5). Only synthetic and open data are used, so there is no
real patient record, no ethics-board approval or data-use agreement is needed, and no personal data
are processed; this is a deliberate design choice. The prototype collects no user accounts or
personal data, holds an entered case in memory only, and serves the API and dashboard on the local
machine only (127.0.0.1). Audit logs of synthetic inputs carry no privacy risk, **a property that
would not hold in a real deployment**. BODHI-S is used under its non-commercial licence, with no text
redistributed (§3.3.2).

**No treatment advice, anywhere.** No rule, template or explanation recommends a treatment, names a
drug or gives a dose; the safety layer removes any sentence that does (FR-6.5).

## 7.11 Checklists before submission

The ethical review checklist of `docs/04` §8 and the data checklist of `docs/03` §7 (§3.7) are
ticked before submission [pending: Phase 4e]: no real patient data at any stage; the disclaimer on
every output path, the report's figures included; no treatment or drug recommendation anywhere; the
red-flag layer independent of the ranker; `degraded_components` shown in the UI; this limitations
chapter present; no claim of clinical efficacy; free text handled as untrusted; and sex and age
breakdowns reported where the sample allows.
