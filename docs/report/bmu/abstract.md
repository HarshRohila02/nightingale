Acute chest pain has dangerous, benign-looking causes. We built Nightingale, a research prototype that ranks 14 possible causes for a clinician; it never gives a diagnosis. A small transformer trained on synthetic DDXPlus patients ranks the causes, a cardiac knowledge graph scores them, a logarithmic opinion pool fuses the two, and independent red-flag rules list dangerous causes first. Evidence passages are shown, never used to rank.

We pre-registered the evaluation, scored every system at 100%, 50% and 25% of each history and opened the test split once (34,657 patients). Six hypotheses are not supported: fusion did not beat its machine-learning component (H1, H1-R) or improve safety (H2, H2-R); grounding did not lower unsupported claims (H3; the language-model explainer failed its checkpoint and does not ship); the transformer did not beat every comparator trained like it (H6). H4, H5 and the Minimum hold, but H4's support is hollow: the graph adds nothing measurable.

At 25% of the history, must-not-miss recall@3 is 0.8710 [0.8659, 0.8764] for the original XGBoost (B1-XGB, `[TEST]`) and 0.9198 [0.9156, 0.9238] for the original logistic regression (B1-LR, `[TEST]`), under the 0.95 target. Every model trained on masked copies meets it (A0′: 0.9986 [0.9980, 0.9991], `[TEST]`).

The data are synthetic, the system knows 14 conditions only, random masks are not history-taking, and no clinician validated any output.
